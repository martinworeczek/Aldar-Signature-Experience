### ★ SIGNAL OF THE WEEK · `e3-s1` Cosm's Shared Reality cinematic experiences
Blooloop · 01 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · [source](https://blooloop.com/cosm-s-shared-reality-cinematic-experiences/) · tag **Retail** · Case study · relevance 4 · experiential 5 · key `d238d408`  
**Lines:** Retail, Hospitality, Community

<img src="https://github.com/martinworeczek/Aldar-Signature-Experience/blob/cxtime/edition-2026-11-02-run3/img/bcce51712b4de104.webp?raw=true" width="360" alt=""> <sub>Image · Blooloop</sub>

*Cosm wraps classic films in 12K LED domes, themed venues and scene-timed menus, giving fans a reason to watch familiar titles together rather than at home.*

- Cosm plays well-known films unchanged at the centre of 87-foot LED domes, while custom screen extensions carry each film's world around and above the audience as the story moves.
- The experience reaches beyond the screen through lighting, sensory touches, interactive installations, and tasting and cocktail menus drawn from each film, served to the seat and timed to scenes.
- Nearly all initial showings of the first title sold out within two weeks and the run was extended twice, and Universal has since joined Warner Bros. as a second studio partner, with a slate spanning sci-fi, family, horror and animation.

**Why it matters to Aldar.** For Retail, it shows how a mall entertainment visit can become a destination worth leaving the sofa for, by building a shared, multi-sensory event around content people already love. For Hospitality, scene-timed themed menus point to F&B that is choreographed with the moment rather than simply served alongside it.

**Signature Experiences to explore:** *Retail:* **Story-wrapped screening nights**: Shoppers book a themed screening at the mall's cinema or event space where lighting, installations and a film-inspired menu turn a classic title into an evening out. · *Hospitality:* **Scene-timed tasting menus**: Guests at a resort dinner-and-film evening receive courses at their seats timed to key scenes, each dish drawn from the story on screen. · *Community:* **Neighbourhood family film fêtes**: Residents gather for an outdoor family screening where the plaza is dressed in the film's world and local vendors serve treats inspired by it.

**Pull-quote:** Cosm leaves the original film untouched and instead enlarges the room around it, so the emotional pull of a familiar story grows when it is shared with a crowd.

### `e3-s2` Climate of Change at OMSI
Blooloop · 01 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · [source](https://blooloop.com/climate-of-change-at-omsi/) · tag **Design** · Case study · relevance 4 · experiential 5 · key `8630ae82`  
**Lines:** Education, Community, Retail

<img src="https://github.com/martinworeczek/Aldar-Signature-Experience/blob/cxtime/edition-2026-11-02-run3/img/beb695bbd0aa70b1.webp?raw=true" width="360" alt=""> <sub>Image · Blooloop</sub>

*Portland's science museum designed a climate exhibition around how visitors feel, not just what they learn, letting them choose their route by mood and readiness.*

- The Oregon Museum of Science and Industry built its bilingual Climate of Change exhibition to address the anxiety and hopelessness people bring to the subject, structuring it as an emotional arc from heart to head to hope to hand.
- Instead of a fixed route, the floor plan, shaped with advice from local Native American youth, opens onto four directions so visitors can pick a theatre of community stories, an immersive space, an art gallery or hands-on science according to whether they feel anxious, energised, curious or reflective.
- Signature moments include a LIDAR-driven projection room where the environment grows from visitors who stand still, a quiet sphere space for reflection, and community-authored content that turns participation into co-ownership.

**Why it matters to Aldar.** For Education, it shows how a school's learning spaces and open days can be designed around a student's or parent's emotional state as much as the content. For Community and Retail, it offers a model for public spaces that let visitors choose a calm or energetic path on each visit.

**Signature Experiences to explore:** *Education:* **Choose-your-path learning hub**: Students entering a school discovery space choose among a quiet reflection corner, a hands-on lab, a story wall of local voices or an art zone, each matched to how ready they feel to engage. · *Community:* **Residents' stories, residents' words**: Residents co-author a rotating community exhibit in a neighbourhood hub, telling local stories of sustainability and resilience in their own voices and seeing themselves reflected in the place. · *Retail:* **Stillness-rewarding installation**: Mall visitors who pause in a responsive projection zone watch landscapes and sound grow outward from where they stand, linking them with others nearby and offering a calm counterpoint to shopping.

**Pull-quote:** OMSI designed the exhibition for the emotional experience of meeting climate change, on the view that feeling shapes a person's willingness to engage.

*Jaguar has turned its London showroom into a destination led by a chef's restaurant, with interiors that echo its new car and private sales spaces below.*

- Jaguar has rebuilt its Knightsbridge flagship as a destination rather than a conventional car showroom, and it is currently the only place where the public can see the new Type 01.
- Most of the ground floor is given to Café Eighty-One, a red-upholstered restaurant by a Michelin-starred chef, with the colour drawn from the area's red-brick streets, while only two cars are on display.

### `e3-s4` Alaska Airlines Flagship Lounge by Graham Baba Architects
Retail Design Blog · 02 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · [source](https://retaildesignblog.net/2026/11/02/alaska-airlines-flagship-lounge-by-graham-baba-architects/) · tag **Design** · Case study · relevance 4 · experiential 4 · key `b4155466`  
**Lines:** Hospitality, Retail

<img src="https://github.com/martinworeczek/Aldar-Signature-Experience/blob/cxtime/edition-2026-11-02-run3/img/d8b972774b1f84b5.webp?raw=true" width="360" alt=""> <sub>Image · Retail Design Blog</sub>

*At Seattle-Tacoma airport, Alaska Airlines moves away from the exclusive business lounge, using a staged entry and four distinct zones to offer every guest a refuge.*

- Graham Baba Architects designed Alaska Airlines' flagship lounge at SeaTac as a warm, welcoming refuge from the concourse, deliberately moving away from the sleek, exclusive model of traditional airport lounges.
- The space is divided into four lightly screened zones (an entry, a casual bistro, a quiet relaxation area and a bar), each with its own furniture, acoustics, materials and lighting so guests can choose the atmosphere that suits them.
- A staged arrival sequence, from a wide concourse window and guiding oak frames to a fireplace view and a concierge desk, is combined with work by local fabricators and artists to root the experience in the Pacific Northwest.

**Why it matters to Aldar.** For Hospitality and Retail, the lounge shows how an arrival sequence and a choice of distinct moods within one space can make a hotel lobby or a mall visitor lounge feel like a welcoming refuge, rather than a transactional waiting area.

**Signature Experiences to explore:** *Hospitality:* **Lobby of four moods**: Guests entering the hotel lobby move from a concierge welcome into lightly screened zones for coffee and conversation, quiet rest with lowered light and soft acoustics, and an evening bar, each with its own materials and seating. · *Retail:* **Take-a-breath mall lounge**: Shoppers can step from a busy concourse into a calm, sound-absorbed lounge with views, recliners and a local coffee counter, open to every visitor rather than reserved for a privileged few.

**Pull-quote:** Alaska Airlines set out to replace the exclusive, business-like lounge with a warm home-away-from-home rooted in the Pacific Northwest, offering a range of spaces so every guest finds one where they feel at home.

### `e3-s6` Light and shadow meet at One Bryant Park
Hospitality Design · 26 Oct 2026 · 2 min read · [source](https://hospitalitydesign.com/news/one-bryant-park-new-york/639478) · tag **Design** · Case study · relevance 3 · experiential 4 · key `6ae05274`  
**Lines:** Hospitality, Retail

<img src="https://github.com/martinworeczek/Aldar-Signature-Experience/blob/cxtime/edition-2026-11-02-run3/img/d4f9efbb9e69cecf.webp?raw=true" width="360" alt=""> <sub>Image · Hospitality Design</sub>

*A new New York restaurant near Times Square uses Japanese ideas of filtered light and preserved shadow to turn three dining rooms into a calm retreat from the street.*

- One Bryant Park on 42nd Street is a single restaurant offering three distinct dining experiences: a main dining room and bar, an omakase counter in Kumiko Room, and Kappo Sono, which is due to open in November.
- Studio Canall designed each room with its own mood, tied together by the interplay of light and shadow and the meeting of Eastern and Western design.
- Light is handled deliberately throughout, filtered through oak slats, backlit kumiko lattice and concealed fixtures, keeping darker pockets so that wood, plaster and metal textures can be felt.

**Why it matters to Aldar.** For Hospitality and mall-based F&B in Retail, it shows how the moment a guest steps in from a busy street can be designed as a shift into calm, using lighting that keeps shadow rather than lighting a room evenly.

**Signature Experiences to explore:** *Hospitality:* **Dappled light dining**: Guests enter a hotel restaurant beneath a slatted timber canopy where light filters through and darker pockets are kept, so the room feels calmer and more intimate after dusk. · *Retail:* **Shade retreat threshold**: Shoppers leave a busy mall concourse through a timber arch into a dining space lit with filtered, uneven light that signals a slower, quieter pause in their visit.

**Pull-quote:** The restaurant was conceived as a retreat from the Times Square bustle, meant to feel like standing in the shade of a forest.
